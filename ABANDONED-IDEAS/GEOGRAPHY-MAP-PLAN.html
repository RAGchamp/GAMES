What is this plan and how does AI play a part?

This page is the build plan for the first phase of Game 3: Geography, the third game in the RAG Champ Games portal. Before there can be a geography game there has to be a good map, so this phase builds a world map with every country, drawn in the bright poster style of the reference picture: coloured countries on a dark navy sea, white name labels with flags and red arrows. Like Car Race and Chintu, everything will be written by an AI coding agent (Claude Code) in plain HTML and JavaScript with the Canvas API. The map shapes come from free public-domain map data, which a small script prepares once. PLEASE NOTE: players don't need any AI to use the map or play the game. The AI is used only to write the code and prepare the data.

🌍 Geography: World Map Plan (Phase 1 of the game)

Plan date: 22 Nov 2026 · Decisions agreed 22 Nov 2026 · Status: Phases 1–5 done 22 Nov 2026 (open the world map); Phase 6 portal card still to do

Goal: Build GAME-3-Geography/WORLD-MAP.html, a map viewer that shows all the world's countries (about 250 map units, see step 2) as a set of poster-style map sheets: one world overview plus one sheet for each continent and region. Every sheet looks like the reference picture: a yellow title banner, brightly coloured countries with a raised relief look, and a white label with the country's flag and a red arrow for each country. Players can pan, zoom and tap any country. This map is the base that the quiz games in later phases will be built on.
1The look we are aiming for

Layout sketch only, with made-up shapes. The real sheets use real country outlines.

The reference picture (a South America poster shared on social media) has seven features that together make the look. We will build each one in code:

  1. Dark navy sea with no grid lines, so the land stands out.
  2. Bright, flat country colours (green, orange, yellow, purple, pink, blue), with no two neighbours sharing a colour.
  3. A raised, textured relief look: mountains and rivers show as light and shade on top of the colour, and the land seems to float a little above the sea.
  4. Thin white borders between countries and a white coastline.
  5. A yellow title banner: heavy yellow capitals in a navy pill with a yellow rim.
  6. White rounded labels with a light-blue rim, the country's flag on the left and its name in bold condensed letters.
  7. Red arrows from each label to its country, arranged so labels never overlap and arrows never cross.

We copy the style, not the picture: the screenshot in GAME-3-Geography/sample-map.jpg is someone else's artwork, so it stays a local reference only and is not used in the game or committed to the repository.

2What "all countries" means
Core
195 countries
The 193 UN member states plus the two UN observer states, Vatican City and Palestine. These are the "countries" the game quizzes on by default.
Also shown
Other places on the map
Taiwan, Kosovo and Western Sahara, and large territories that look like countries on a map, such as Greenland, French Guiana (it is on the reference picture), Puerto Rico, New Caledonia and the Falkland Islands. They are shown with their own colour and a label that says who governs them.
Tiny places
Always visible and tappable
Countries smaller than a few pixels on screen (Vatican City, Monaco, the Maldives, Tuvalu, the Caribbean islands) get a coloured ring marker so they can still be seen, labelled and tapped.

Every country gets one record with: ISO code, short name, official name, capital, continent, region sheet, flag, label anchor point and map colour. A coverage check page (Phase 6) lists all records and flags any country that is missing a shape, a flag or a label.

5How each part of the look is drawn
Projection
Flat map from a round Earth
The world sheet uses the Equal Earth projection (nicely rounded, true sizes). Region sheets use a Lambert azimuthal view centred on the region, like an atlas page. Both are about 60 lines of our own maths, with no library.
Colours
Neighbours always differ
The build script knows which countries touch and gives each one a colour from a six-colour palette so that no two neighbours match. Colours are fixed in the data, so Brazil is always the same green.
Relief
Light and shade on the land
The grey hillshade picture is re-projected to match each sheet, clipped to the land and blended over the colours with a "multiply and lighten" mix. A soft shadow under the coast makes the land look raised.
Borders and coast
White lines
Thin white country borders. The coastline gets a thicker white line with a faint blue glow on the sea side, as in the picture.
Labels
Flag pills around the edge
Each country has a label point deep inside it. Labels are placed in columns on the left and right (and top or bottom when needed) of the land, sorted from top to bottom so arrows do not cross. A tidy-up pass pushes labels apart until none overlap.
Speed
Draw once, reuse
The coloured, shaded land is drawn once into a hidden canvas for each sheet and zoom level. Panning and tapping then only redraw the labels and highlights, so it stays smooth on phones.

6What players can do with the map
  • Pick a sheet from a menu, or tap a continent on the world sheet to fly in.
  • Pan and zoom with drag, the mouse wheel or a two-finger pinch. Double-tap zooms in.
  • Tap a country to highlight it (it glows and lifts) and open a card with its flag, name, capital and continent.
  • Search by typing a country name. The map moves to that country and highlights it.
  • Labels on or off: hiding the labels gives a blank map, which later becomes the quiz mode.
  • Save as picture: download the current sheet as a PNG poster.
  • Phones: portrait shows the whole sheet; landscape shows the sheet with the menu at the side. Tap targets are at least 44 px.
  • Keyboard: arrow keys pan, +/− zoom, L toggles labels, / opens search.

Build phases

Phase 1: Map data pipeline ✅ done 22 Nov 2026

  • Write tools/build-map-data.mjs (Node.js 24 is already installed): download, clean, simplify and pack the Natural Earth shapes as in step 3.
  • Build the country list (step 2) with names, capitals, continents and region sheets, and fix the known awkward cases: French Guiana, Kosovo, Western Sahara, Northern Cyprus, Somaliland and countries split by the date line (Russia, Fiji, Kiribati).
  • Work out neighbours, pick map colours and find each country's label point.
  • Built: tools/build-map-data.mjs merges the two Natural Earth files at 1:10m and simplifies them to about 2 km detail. India's view is used for India, Pakistan, China, Ukraine, Russia, Israel, Lebanon, Sudan, Brazil, Colombia, Greenland and Kazakhstan. It is not used for Cyprus, Morocco, Serbia and Somalia, because there it would hide Northern Cyprus, Western Sahara, Kosovo and Somaliland, which were agreed to be shown. Result: 195 countries, 45 territories and 21 unlabelled areas in a 0.9 MB file. Every UN country is present, no neighbours share a colour, and the colours are used evenly (44/44/44/43/43/43).
Done when: The script runs with one command, every one of the ~250 map units has a valid shape and a record, no two neighbours share a colour, and the shapes file is under 1.5 MB.

9Test checklist
  • Automatic: every country has a shape, a record, a flag and a sheet; no two neighbours share a colour; no labels overlap; no arrows cross; every arrow tip lies inside its country.
  • Opens from file:/// in Chrome, Edge and Firefox with no console errors.
  • Countries crossing the date line (Russia, Fiji) draw without stray lines across the map.
  • Tapping each tiny country (Vatican City, Monaco, Nauru, Tuvalu) selects it, not its neighbour or the sea.
  • The map stays sharp when zoomed in and when a phone is rotated.
  • Spot checks by a person: 20 random countries compared with an atlas for shape, name, capital and flag.
10Decisions (agreed 22 Nov 2026)
  • Country list: 195 plus extras shown. The 195 countries from step 2 count as "countries" in quizzes. Taiwan, Kosovo, Western Sahara and the territories (Greenland, French Guiana and others) are drawn and labelled on the map but are optional in quizzes.
  • Disputed borders: India's official view. Borders follow Natural Earth's India point-of-view version (for example, all of Jammu and Kashmir, including Ladakh, is shown as part of India). Overseas parts such as French Guiana are still split out as in step 3. The country card adds a short note where a border is disputed.
  • Relief: the real hillshade picture (step 5), blended over the colours, because it looks most like the reference.
  • Flags: the flag-icons set packed into one sprite. Flag emoji are not used, because Windows shows them as two letters instead of a flag.
  • Data in separate files. The generated data lives in data/*.js next to the page, not inside one huge HTML file. This is unlike Car Race and Chintu, which are single files. The map still opens with a double-click.
  • Names: short English. Labels use short, familiar English names ("Ivory Coast", "Czechia", "Myanmar"), and the country card shows the official name.

What comes after this plan: once the map is done, later phases turn it into the game. Ideas: "Tap the country" quizzes, dragging the flag labels onto the right arrows (the reference look suits this well), flag matching and a timed "fill the continent" challenge. They will get their own plan.
