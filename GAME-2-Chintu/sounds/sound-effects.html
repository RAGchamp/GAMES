🔊 Chintu Sound Effects

Every sound in Chintu, what it is for, and how the game makes it. Press ▶︎ Play to hear one. Game plan

What is this and how does AI play a part?

Chintu has no sound files except one ship-horn recording. Every other sound effect and all the music are made by code while the game runs, using the browser's Web Audio API: a few lines describe a wave, its pitch and how its volume fades. Claude Code (an AI coding agent) wrote the sounds; this page shows each recipe so you can learn from it, try changes and copy a better version back into the game.

1How the game makes a sound
A sound is a wave (square, triangle, sine or sawtooth, or random noise), with a starting pitch that may slide to another pitch, a volume that jumps up in 5 ms and fades away, and a length. Play a few short ones in a row and you have a tune.
The 5 building blocks (the same as in chintu.html)
CallWhat it does
tone(f, dur, o)One note. f = start pitch in hertz (Hz), dur = length in seconds. Options o: to = pitch to slide to, type = wave, vol = loudness (0 to 1), at = wait this many seconds first.
noise(dur, o)A burst of hiss passed through a filter. filter = 'lowpass' (rumble), 'highpass' (sizzle) or 'bandpass' (whoosh); freq = filter pitch; to = filter slides here; vol; at.
arp(notes, step, o)Plays a list of notes one after another, step seconds apart. Notes are MIDI numbers (60 = middle C, +12 = one octave up). len = note length, at0 = start delay; other options as in tone.
mid(n)Turns a MIDI note number into hertz: mid(69) = 440 Hz (the A above middle C).
horn(rate, vol, at)Plays the ship-horn recording. rate 1 = normal; lower is deeper and longer, higher is shorter and higher.

8Music loops

Each loop is built from 4 chords, one per bar, 16 steps per bar. A lead plays notes picked from the chord (pattern a for bars 1 to 4, b for bars 5 to 8), a triangle-wave bass plays the chord's root, and there is a kick, snare and hi-hat made of noise. The numbers in a and b pick a chord note: 0 = root, 1 = third, 2 = fifth, 3 to 5 = the same an octave higher; _ is a rest. Bass numbers are semitones above the root. After 8 bars the loop starts again.

9Future sounds for new characters in later versions

These sounds are not in the game yet. They are ready for the characters we add in the next versions. Each one is made from a recording in this sounds folder, prepared the same way as the ship horn: the best phrase was cut out at a quiet moment, mixed to mono, low rumble removed from the voices, given soft fades and made the same loudness. The prepared clips are saved in sounds/custom/ (future-maleLove.wav, future-maleLoveLong.wav, future-femaleLove.wav, future-guitar.wav, future-tuba.wav) and are also built into this page. The custom sounds below change those clips while they play: faster or slower (higher or lower), filtered, with echo, backwards, or only a part of a clip.

CallWhat it does
sample(name, o)Plays a prepared clip: 'maleLove', 'maleLoveLong', 'femaleLove', 'guitar' or 'tuba'. Options o: rate = speed and pitch (1 = normal, 2 = an octave higher and twice as fast, 0.5 = an octave lower); start and dur = which part of the clip, in seconds; vol (0.8 if not given); at = wait first; filter = 'lowpass', 'highpass' or 'bandpass' with freq and q; echo = seconds between echoes (with feedback, 0.35 if not given); reverse: true = backwards.

Not used: kaante-nahi-kathte.wav is a song from the film Mr. India. It is copyrighted, so it is left out of the game and this page.

✏️How to change a sound
  1. Edit the code in a box (it turns yellow when changed) and press ▶︎ Play to hear it. Press ↺ Reset to go back to the game's version.
  2. When you like it, open GAME-2-Chintu/chintu.html, search for the sound's name followed by a colon (for example jump:) in the FX list, and put your code after the () =>. Code with more than one line goes inside { … }.
  3. Music loops live in the TRACKS list just below FX.
  4. To bring a future sound into the game: embed its clip from sounds/custom/ the same way as the horn (as a base64 string), copy the sample() helper from this page into the game's sound block, and add an FX entry with the code from its box.
Quick ideas
To make it…Change
Lower or higherHalve or double f and to (one octave), or add/subtract 12 from MIDI notes.
Softer or brightertype: 'sine' is the softest, then 'triangle', 'square', and 'sawtooth' is the buzziest.
Longer or snappierChange dur (or step and len in arp).
Rise instead of fallSwap f and to.
ThickerAdd a second tone an octave below at lower vol, or a short noise for a hit.
Quieter in the gameLower vol. Keep effects at about 0.3 or less, so they do not drown the music.
