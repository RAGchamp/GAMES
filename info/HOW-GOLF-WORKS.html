1The course: 18 holes from short recipes

The course is real size: the holes run from 135 m to 521 m, with four par 3s, ten par 4s and four par 5s. The front nine is parkland; the back nine runs along the sea, where the far side of the rough drops away and a ball there is out of bounds. Each hole is written as a short recipe, and the game builds the whole map from it: the ground's height, the fairway, rough, bunkers, water, trees and the green.

Hole 5, "Left Turn", as it is written in golf.html 
{ n: 5, par: 4, name: 'Left Turn',
  cl: [[0, 0], [190, 0], [300, 45]], // the centre line: a dogleg left
  fw: 34,                            // fairway width (m)
  gr: 15, tilt: [0.008, -0.01],      // green radius and slope
  cup: [2, -3],                      // the cup, from the green's middle
  hills:   [[80, -25, 35, 3], ...],  // along, across, size, height
  bunkers: [[200, -16, 8, 4], ...],
  trees:   [[205, 26], [214, 36], ...] }

Trees are added along both edges of the rough by a seeded random pattern, so a hole always looks the same. The ground is gentle rolls everywhere, plus the hills, with a smooth tilted plane under each green and banks rising beyond the rough.

2The clubs, and how the ball flies

Each club has its own range of angles, its own top speed at full power, and its own spin, which decides how softly the ball lands. Power sets the speed from nothing to the club's top speed. Once the ball leaves the club, three things act on it: gravity pulls it down, air drag slows it (more at high speed), and wind pushes it, because drag works on the ball's speed through the air, not over the ground.

3Wind that keeps you guessing

Every hole starts with a new random wind: a speed from 0 to 8 m/s (in half steps) and any compass direction. If a hole has been in play for 3 minutes, the wind changes again, and every 3 minutes after that, but never while the ball is moving, so a shot is never spoiled by a change you couldn't see. A tailwind carries the ball further, a headwind holds it up, and a crosswind curves it sideways, so you aim off. Putts are not touched by the wind. Easy halves the wind and gives a longer aim guide; the practice range has no wind at all.

Try it: a full 5-Iron, seen from above

// at the start of every hole, and after 3 minutes
function newWind() {
  wind.speed = round(random() * 16) / 2;  // 0 to 8 m/s
  if (easy) wind.speed /= 2;
  wind.dir = random() * 360;
  wind.next = holeTime + 180;            // 3 minutes
}
// each frame: change only when the ball is still
if (holeTime >= wind.next && ballStopped) newWind();

High shots stay in the air longer, so the wind moves them more: a Wedge in a crosswind drifts much further for its length than a Driver.

4Bounce, roll, and getting it in the cup

When the ball lands, the game works out the slope under it. The part of its speed into the ground bounces back (28% on the fairway, 22% on the green), and the part along the ground is kept by the surface and the club's spin, which is why a Wedge stops quickly and a Driver runs on. When a bounce is too small, the ball rolls: the slope pulls it downhill and the grass slows it, fast on the fairway and only a little on the smooth green.

Try it: a putt on a sloping green

// rolling: each tiny step
v -= 9.8 * slope * dt;            // downhill pull
v *= max(0, |v| - MU * 9.8 * dt) / |v|;  // grass
// the green: MU = 0.065; the fairway: 0.30

// the cup has a radius of 5.4 cm
if (passes within 1.35 cup radii && speed < 1.7 m/s)
  in!                     // (2 radii if very slow)
else if (crosses the cup && speed < 3 m/s)
  lip-out: turned aside and slowed

A ball that stops very close to the cup (75 cm, or 1.5 m on Easy) is tapped in for one more stroke, and a hole ends after 10 strokes ("picked up"), so a bad hole never gets stuck.

5Scoring, the two views and Willy
Scoring
  • Par is what a good golfer needs. One under is a birdie, two under an eagle, one in is a hole in one; one over a bogey, two over a double bogey.
  • The scorecard shows every hole, with circles round birdies and squares round bogeys, and the round as a total such as "+3".
  • Your best round and each hole's best are saved on the device.
Two views (⇄ View or V)
  • Side view: the ground along your aim line, with its real hills, so you see how high the ball flies and where it bounces.
  • Top-down view: the hole from above, with its bends, trees, bunkers and water; drag to aim. On the green, arrows show the slope.
  • Switch at any time, even mid-flight: both draw the same ball from the same rules.
Willy, sound and music
  • Willy is drawn in code from a reference picture: cream cap, red polo shirt and red shoes. He addresses the ball face-on, swings back over his shoulder and follows through, and cheers a birdie.
  • Every sound and the music are made by the browser's Web Audio, so the game has no sound files.

How it was checked
  • Club distances: in Node, each club's carry at full power was measured with the game's own flight: 199, 176, 144, 105 and 70 m, all within 2% of the plan's table.
  • The same shot always lands in the same place, so the aim guide and the club suggestion can be trusted.
  • A solver plays every hole in Node, trying many clubs, angles, powers and aims for each shot. With no wind it finished every hole at par or better (it plays perfectly, so it was well under par).

  • People played it on a laptop and a phone, which led to Willy's face-on swing, the red shoes and the bigger ball.
