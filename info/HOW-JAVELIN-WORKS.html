What is this page and how does AI play a part?

This page explains how Game 3: Javelin works inside: how two sliders turn into a javelin flying along a real arc, how the towers wobble, break and fall, how the wind and the rising balloons work, and what Claude Code (an AI coding agent) built. Claude Code wrote the whole game from a short plan and one picture of Rocky, and a person tested every version on a computer and a phone. Players don't need any AI to play. The demos on this page use the same formulas and numbers as the game, so you can try them yourself.

1How a throw becomes a flight path

When you press THROW, Rocky runs up and lets go of the javelin from his raised hand, 3.5 m above the ground. The two sliders decide how it leaves his hand: Angle is the direction (5° is nearly flat, 85° nearly straight up) and Pace is the speed, from 8 m/s at 0% to 22 m/s at 100%. After that, Rocky can't change anything: three forces decide where it goes.

The three forces on a flying javelin

Gravity pulls it down all the time, so the path bends into an arc. Drag is the air pushing back; it grows with speed and points backwards along the path, so fast throws lose more. Wind pushes sideways: a tailwind (→) carries the javelin further, a headwind (←) blows it back.

The flight in code (from javelin.html) 
// Pace 0..100% becomes 8..22 m/s (Heavy x0.88, Feather x1.12)
speed = (8 + pace * (22 - 8)) * type.speed;
vx = speed * cos(angle);  vy = speed * sin(angle);

// every 1/240 of a second (4 small steps per frame):
sp  = Math.hypot(vx, vy);
vx += (-0.0035 * drag * sp * vx + 0.3 * windEffect * wind) * h;
vy += (-9.8    - 0.0035 * drag * sp * vy) * h;
x  += vx * h;   y += vy * h;

That's the whole flight. Each small step nudges the speed (gravity, drag, wind), then moves the javelin a little. The javelin always points along its path, so it flies nose-first and comes down tip-first. If it lands too flat (less than about 11° downwards), it skids and doesn't leave a mark.

Aim guideThe dotted line in front of Rocky uses this same step to look ahead. It shows the first quarter of the path (half with Easy on), so you still have to judge the rest.

2Four javelins, four numbers each

3Towers that wobble, break and fall

The towers are built with Matter.js, a free open-source 2D physics library (MIT licence). Every crate, post, slab, target and trophy cup is a solid body with a weight, friction and bounce. Matter.js works out how they push on each other, slide, tip over and fall 60 times a second. Its code is pasted into the game as one long line, so the game needs no internet connection to play.

The javelin itself isn't a Matter.js body while it flies; it uses the flight code from section 1. When its tip touches something, the game decides what happens:

  • Glass always shatters, and the javelin flies on, a little slower.
  • Wood loses strength: damage = speed × 3.5 × the javelin's damage. Out of strength, it splinters and the javelin goes through; otherwise the javelin sticks in it and gives it a shove.
  • Stone only the Heavy javelin can break. Other javelins bounce off.
  • Steel-rim boards can't break. They need 2 hits, or you knock them over.
  • A shove is a push at the exact point that was hit, so hitting high on a tower tips it over and hitting low slides it.

Pick a javelin and how fast it is going when it hits. One hit is shown, using the game's strength and damage numbers.

No falling before you throw. When a level starts, every piece is put to sleep: Matter.js leaves it alone until something touches it. A test program also wakes every piece of every level and checks that each tower still stands on its own.

Stars. Clear every target to win. You get ★ for clearing the level, ★★ with 1 javelin left, and ★★★ with 2 left or a high enough score. Each javelin left over adds 2,000 points.

4Levels, wind and Just Balloons

Every level is a short recipe with distances in metres from Rocky. Small helpers build the parts: box, frame (two posts and a slab), board, cup, balloon, bell, mover (a target that slides on a rail) and swing (a sign on a rope).

// level 2-4 "High Wind" (from javelin.html)
{ id: '2-4', name: 'High Wind', jav: 4, wind: 4, build: b => {
  let t = b.frame(34, 0, 2.4, 2);       // posts + slab at 34 m
  t = b.frame(34, t, 2.4, 2, 'glass');  // a glass floor on top
  b.board(34, t);                       // the target on the top
  b.balloon(26, 9, { drift: 1 }); } }

Wind in the levels is fixed per ground (World 2), changes after every throw (World 3's gusts), or is light (World 4). Easy halves it. In Practice field and Just Balloons you choose: tap the windsock, or press N, for no wind, light (1 to 2 m/s), strong (3 to 4.5 m/s) or random (a new wind after every throw).

Just Balloons has no tower. 4 balloons rise into the sky like real ones let go: each one speeds up for its first second, climbs at its own speed (0.9 to 1.7 m/s) on its own slant (up to about 30° either way), wanders a little, tilts on its string, and drifts further and further with the wind. One that floats out of the picture comes back up from the grass somewhere new. Each balloon starts at a spot that some throw can reach in the current wind, so every round can be won, but you have to aim where it will be. Balloons in the levels fly too: each one rises (0.8 to 1.2 m/s) through the spot the level puts it, floats out of the top, and comes back up from the grass on the same line, so you can wait for it to come round again.

5Rocky, drawing and sound, all with code

Rocky is drawn with canvas shapes from one picture of him: swept-up brown hair, a vest and shorts, socks and shoes. His arms and legs are moved by a few angles each frame for standing, the run-up, the throw and the follow-through. He is drawn 1.6 times life size so he's easy to see on a phone. The throw is overarm: his hand draws the javelin back behind his head, whips it up and forward, and lets go at the exact point (3.5 m up) where the flight starts, so the javelin never jumps.

The picture fits any screen. The camera fits the whole level in, from Rocky to the furthest target, and a taller screen just shows more sky. There are distance markers every 5 m, wood splinters, glass shards, dust, confetti, screen shake, and a last-throw "ghost" path to help you adjust.

Sound. No sound files: everything is made with the browser's Web Audio. Each of the 24 sounds (footsteps, the whoosh, the thunk of a javelin sticking in, glass, wood, stone, steel, bells, balloon pops, the crowd cheer and more) is a short mix of tones and noise, shaped by filters and volume curves. A small stadium music loop is played from a list of notes. Nothing sounds until the first click or tap, and Sound and Music can be switched off.

Controls work with a mouse, a finger or the keys: ↑↓ angle, ←→ pace, Space throw, 1–4 javelin, R restart, P pause, F full screen, H home, N wind. Stars, best scores and settings are saved in the browser.

6What Claude Code built, and how it was checked

Unlike Car Race, Javelin didn't start from someone else's example. Claude Code wrote it from a plan and one picture. The only borrowed code is the Matter.js physics library. The game file has three scripts:

sim: the rulesFlight, levels, breaking, scoring, wind and balloons. It draws nothing, so a test program can play every level without a browser.
→
game-ui: the screenDrawing, Rocky, menus, sliders, keys, sound and saving. It asks the rules what happened and shows it.
←
matter: the physics libraryMatter.js 0.19.0, pasted in unchanged, which moves and stacks the tower pieces.

How it was checked
  • A computer solver plays every level with the real rules, trying angles, paces, javelins and (for moving targets) waiting times. After every change to the flight it was run again: all 31 levels can be cleared within their javelin limits.
  • A tower test wakes every piece of every level and checks nothing falls over on its own.
  • A balloon test plays rounds of Just Balloons in every wind and pops each balloon with one real throw, aimed where the balloon will be.
  • A test browser opens the game, throws, and takes pictures frame by frame (for example, to check the throw looks overarm), and checks there are no errors.
  • People played it on a laptop and on a phone, which led to the bigger Rocky, the overarm throw, Home, the wind button and the rising balloons.
