What is this plan and how does AI play a part?

This page is the build plan for Game 5: Billiards, the fifth game in the RAG Champ Games portal. It is English billiards, the old British game played with just three balls on a full-size snooker table. Like the other games, it will be written by an AI coding agent (Claude Code) as one HTML file with plain JavaScript and the Canvas API. There are no frameworks, no build step and no server. It reuses the tested parts of Golf and Javelin: the sim / game-ui split, the fixed-step loop, the sliders, the sound and the way the game is tested. PLEASE NOTE: players don't need any AI to play the game. The AI is used only to write the code (and to plan the computer opponent's shots, which is ordinary code, not AI).

Goal: Build GAME-5-Billiards/billiards.html, a game of English billiards seen from above on a 12 ft × 6 ft table with six pockets. There are three balls: a white, a yellow and a red. You play the white and the computer plays the yellow; each is that side's own cue ball. You score by potting a ball, by going in-off (your cue ball goes into a pocket after hitting another ball) and by making a cannon (your cue ball hits both other balls). You keep playing while you score; the first to the target score (50 by default) wins. You play against the computer, and both sides are shown as players standing at the table. There are two views, switched at any time as in Golf: Top (the default) and the Eye view, low behind the cue ball, the way the player sees the table before striking.

1The three balls, and whose ball is whose
BallBelongs toStarts
WhiteYour cue ballIn hand: played from the "D"
YellowThe computer's cue ballOff the table until the computer's first turn, then played from the D
RedNobody: both players play at itOn the billiard spot, near the top cushion

The yellow takes the place of the classic dotted (spot) white, and is easier to tell apart on a small screen. It is not a third object ball: it is the computer's cue ball. On your turn you strike only the white, and the other two balls (the red and the yellow) are the balls you play at; on the computer's turn it strikes the yellow and plays at the red and the white. Who goes first is decided by a coin toss.

  • The D and baulk: a line across the table near the bottom end is the baulk line, with a half-circle on it, the D. A cue ball "in hand" is placed anywhere in the D.
  • Spots: the billiard spot (near the top cushion), the pyramid spot (halfway between the centre and the top cushion) and the centre spot. A potted red goes back on the billiard spot; if that is covered, on the pyramid spot; if that is covered too, on the centre spot.
  • A potted opponent's ball (the yellow on your turn, the white on the computer's) stays off the table until its owner's next turn, when it is played from the D.
  • After an in-off, your own cue ball comes back "in hand" and you play your next shot from the D.
  • Playing out of baulk: when you play from hand, your cue ball must first cross the baulk line before it touches a ball that is in baulk. The game shows this with a shaded baulk area and a warning.

ScoreWhat happensPoints
Red pot (red winning hazard)Your cue ball hits the red into a pocket3
Opponent's ball pot (white winning hazard)Your cue ball hits your opponent's cue ball into a pocket2
In-off red (red losing hazard)Your cue ball hits the red first, then goes into a pocket3
In-off opponent's ball (white losing hazard)Your cue ball hits your opponent's cue ball first, then goes into a pocket2
CannonYour cue ball hits both other balls, in either order2

Scores in one shot add up: a cannon that also pots the red is 2 + 3 = 5. The most from one shot is 10 (cannon, both balls potted, and in-off). After an in-off that also made a cannon, the in-off counts as off the ball hit first; if both were hit at the same moment, it counts as off the opponent's ball (2). (The official names say "white" for the opponent's ball, from the days of two whites; in this game that ball is the yellow on your turn.)

  • Your turn goes on while every shot scores; a shot that scores nothing (or a foul) ends your turn.
  • The break counter shows your current break ("Break 23"); the best break of the game and of all time are saved.
  • Winning: the first to reach the target score. 50 up is the default (about ten minutes); 100 and 200 up are in the menu.

Left out: the match limits that stop endless repeat scores in professional play (caps on hazards or cannons in a row, and the red moving to the centre spot after repeated pots off its spot). At 50 up they would hardly ever matter.

3How a turn plays
  1. Place the ball if it is in hand: drag your white anywhere inside the D.
  2. Aim: drag anywhere on the table to turn the cue around your ball, or use ←→ (hold Shift for fine aim). A fine-aim wheel turns the cue by a tenth of a degree for thin cuts.
  3. Read the guide: a dotted line shows where your ball goes, a faint ghost ball shows where it will touch the first ball, and a short line shows which way that ball will go. The guide stops at the first ball (Easy shows one cushion bounce too).
  4. Choose spin by tapping a spot on the big cue-ball picture: above the middle for top (the ball follows on), below for screw (it comes back), left or right for side (it bends off cushions).
  5. Set Power with the slider, or by pulling the cue back on the table, and press SHOOT (or Space).
  6. Watch the balls roll, hit and bounce. The score for the shot pops up ("Cannon + red pot: 5") and is added to your break.
Computer opponent
  • It tries many shots in the same sim as the real game (different angles, powers and spins), scores each one by the points it makes and where it leaves the balls for the next shot, and plays the best.
  • Easy tries few shots and adds a big wobble to its aim; Medium a small wobble; Hard plans two shots ahead and plays safe (leaves the balls hard for you) when it can't score.
  • It "thinks" for a moment with the cue moving, so the player can see what it is aiming at.
Players at the table
  • Two characters drawn in code stand around the table: Archie (you, white ball) and Rusty (the computer, yellow ball). See "Meet Archie and Rusty" below. The one whose turn it is walks to the cue ball, bends over the cue and strikes; the other waits by the wall.
  • So they never hide a ball, the player is drawn behind the cue and turns see-through wherever they cover the table; a "Hide players" switch removes them.
  • Small reactions: a nod for a good pot, a head shake after a miss, a bow on winning.

4bTwo views: Top and Eye view

As in Golf, a ⇄ View button (or V) switches between two views at any time: before, during or after a shot. Top is the default and every game starts in it. The Eye view shows the table the way the player sees it, bent over the cue just before striking. Both views draw the same balls from the same sim, so switching never changes or resets anything.

Mock-up of the eye view, not the final art: Archie is lining up a long, straight pot on the red into the far middle of the top cushion, with the yellow off to the right. The same controls, HUD and corner buttons as in Top view.

  • Where the eye is: just behind the cue ball, on the aim line, low over the cue (about 30 cm above the cloth), looking along the shot. It turns with the aim, so turning left and right swings the whole table round, as when a real player moves round the ball.

  • What you see: the cloth, cushions and pockets in perspective, the balls getting smaller with distance, their shadows under the lamp, and the aim guide and ghost ball. At the bottom are Archie's bridge hand and the cue tip, pulling back as you set Power and striking on SHOOT.
  • During the shot the eye stays where it was, like a player watching from the shot position; switch to Top at any moment to follow every ball.
  • Rusty's turn: the eye view moves behind Rusty's cue ball, so you see what he is aiming at while he lines up.
  • Aiming is the same in both views: drag left or right, the arrow keys and the fine-aim wheel all turn the cue, and placing a ball in hand happens in Top view (the game switches for you, then back).

How it is drawn (changed 23 Nov 2026: three.js): the Eye view is drawn in 3D with three.js (r160, MIT licence): the table under a lamp, shiny balls with soft shadows, and the cue resting on a 3D bridge hand. three.js is pasted into the file as plain text and run only when the Eye view is first opened, so the game still needs no network and the Top view pays nothing for it. The guide and spin hint are drawn over it through the same camera. If a device has no WebGL, the game uses the first plan instead: a simple hand-written 3D projection, like Golf's views. Each point on the table (corners, cushion edges, pocket rims, the baulk line and D, the guide) is turned into screen position by the camera's position and direction; things are drawn from far to near; a ball is a shaded circle whose size depends on its distance, with an oval shadow below it. Anything behind the eye is left out.

The game works in metres, so every distance is real: a ball rolling at 1 m/s on real cloth stops after about 2.5 m, and the game should match that.

Billiards is all about position play: scoring, and leaving the balls where you can score again. The sizes of the pockets and the friction of the cloth decide how hard or easy that is, so they are tuned in Phase 1 with the "Practice table" (balls placed by hand, no scoring).

6How the balls move (the physics)
  • Sliding, then rolling. A struck ball first slides across the cloth; friction turns its slide into a roll after a short distance. This is what makes top (follow) and screw (draw back) work: a ball with backspin keeps spinning backwards after it stops the object ball, and pulls itself back.
  • Ball meets ball. Almost-perfect bounce (balls keep about 95% of their energy). The hit ball goes off along the line between the two centres; the cue ball goes off at a right angle to that (the "90° rule"), and then spin bends its path.
  • Cushions. A ball bounces back with about 75% of its speed across the cushion. Side spin changes the angle it comes off at, and running side makes it come off longer.
  • Pockets. Each pocket has two curved jaws (the rounded ends of the cushions) and a hole. A ball that hits a jaw can rattle and stay out, as in real play; a ball whose centre passes over the hole drops in.
  • No skipping through. Each frame is split into small steps, and the time of every hit is worked out exactly, so even a fast ball never goes through another ball or a cushion.
  • No outside physics library. Matter.js (used in Javelin) handles boxes and stacks, not rolling balls with spin, so the ball physics is written by hand in the sim script.
Each small step (planned code) 
// for every ball still moving:
slip = ball.vel - ball.spin x R;      // is it sliding?
if (|slip| > 0.01) {
  ball.vel  -= MU_SLIDE * g * dir(slip) * dt;
  ball.spin += ...;                  // cloth turns slide into roll
} else {
  ball.vel  -= MU_ROLL * g * dir(vel) * dt;   // rolling slows gently
}
ball.pos += ball.vel * dt;
// then: find the first hit (ball, cushion, jaw, pocket)
// inside this step, move to it, bounce, repeat.
// The shot ends when every ball is still.

Starting numbers (tuned in Phase 1): sliding friction 0.2, rolling friction 0.01, ball-on-ball bounce 0.95, cushion bounce 0.75. The same shot with the same settings always gives exactly the same result, so replays and the computer player's planning match what really happens.

  • Units: metres, seconds and radians. The origin is the middle of the bottom (baulk) cushion; the table runs "up" to the top cushion.
  • The shot record: every shot produces a list of events such as {t:0.42, hit:'red'}, {t:0.90, cushion:'left'}, {t:1.31, hit:'spot'}, {t:2.05, pocket:'top-right', ball:'red'}. The referee reads only this list, so the rules can be tested without drawing anything.
  • Game state (ball positions, scores, whose turn, which ball is in hand, the break) is one plain object that is saved after every shot, so leaving and coming back carries on the game.
  • Drawing: in Top view the table is drawn once to an off-screen canvas; each frame draws only the balls, the cue and the guide on top, so it stays smooth on phones. The Eye view redraws the table each frame through the 3D projection (a few dozen shapes, cheap on a phone). The rules (sim) don't know which view is showing.
  • No outside files, no network requests, works from file:///.

Build phases

Phase 1: The table and the balls

  • Canvas, fixed-step loop and the sim / game-ui split, copied from Golf.
  • The table in real sizes, pockets with jaws, the baulk line, the D and the spots.
  • Ball physics: sliding and rolling, ball-on-ball, cushions, pockets, and exact hit times.
  • Aiming, fine aim, the Power slider, SHOOT, and the aim guide with the ghost ball. No spin yet.
  • A Practice table: drag any ball anywhere and shoot, no rules.
Done when: The same shot always gives the same result, balls never pass through each other or the cushions even at full power, a straight full-ball hit stops the cue ball dead (with no spin), and a ball sent along a cushion into a corner pocket drops in.

Phase 2: The rules and turns

  • The shot record and the referee: pots, in-offs, cannons, adding up, fouls, re-spotting the red, a potted cue ball staying off, ball in hand, playing out of baulk.
  • Turns, breaks, the coin toss, target score (50 by default, 100, 200), win screen.
  • A simple stand-in computer (plays a plain shot at the nearer ball) so whole games can be played before Phase 4.
  • Saving a game in progress, best break and wins.
  • Node tests: a list of set-up shots, each with the points and fouls the referee must give.
Done when: Every row of the scoring and foul tables has a Node test that passes, including the 10-point shot, and a full game to 50 can be played against the stand-in computer.

Phase 3: Spin and the Eye view

  • The Eye view: the 3D projection, the camera behind the cue ball turning with the aim, raising and lowering the eye, the bridge hand and cue, and the ⇄ View button and V key (Top stays the default).
  • The spin picker; top, screw and side in the physics.
  • Side changing cushion angles; the guide hints at the effect on Easy.
  • A "Spin school" practice set: five set-up shots that need follow, screw or side, with a target circle to land the cue ball in.
Done when: A full-ball screw shot brings the cue ball back towards the player, top makes it follow through, side visibly changes the angle off a cushion, and every Spin school shot can be made. In the Eye view the aim guide lines up with the same ball as in Top view, and a shot can be aimed and played from the Eye view alone.

Built 23 Nov 2026. The spin hint (a dotted line showing where the white runs after its first contact) is shown on every shot for now, since the levels come in Phase 4; Phase 4 keeps it for Easy only. Side changes only the angle off a cushion: a ball with no side bounces exactly as in Phase 2.

Phase 4: The computer opponent

  • Shot search in the sim: many angles, powers and spins, each scored by points and the leave.
  • Easy, Medium and Hard, with aim wobble and, on Hard, two-shot planning and safety play.
  • "Thinking" animation, and the search spread over several frames so the screen never freezes.
Done when: In Node, Hard scores at least 3 times as many points as Easy over 50 games against itself, Medium beats Easy most of the time, and no computer shot takes more than 2 seconds to choose on a phone.

Built 23 Nov 2026. In Node, over 50 games Hard v Easy, Hard scored 2,575 points to Easy's 378 (6.8 times) and won all 50; over 51 games Medium v Easy, Medium won 50. On a laptop Hard takes about 0.36 s to choose a shot (0.72 s at most in a timed run); in the game Rusty plays his best shot so far after 1.5 s of thinking, so a slow phone never waits much more than 2 s. Rusty's search uses the same sim as the game; a quick test that skips cushions a ball cannot reach made the sim about 4 times faster with exactly the same results. The spin hint is shown in a game on Easy only (always on the Practice table and in Spin school).

9Test checklist
  • Opens from file:/// in Chrome, Edge and Firefox with no console errors.
  • The same shot always gives the same result; a replay matches the shot exactly.
  • No ball ever leaves the table, overlaps another ball or passes through a cushion, over 10,000 random full-power shots in Node.
  • Every scoring and foul rule has a Node test, including combined scores and the in-off-after-cannon rule.
  • The red goes to the right spot when its spot is covered; a potted cue ball (white or yellow) stays off until its owner's turn.
  • Playing from hand: the ball can only be placed in the D, and a direct shot at a ball in baulk is called a foul.
  • Scores, breaks and the winner are right at the end of a game.
  • Leaving mid-game and coming back keeps the balls, scores and whose turn it is.
  • Switching tabs pauses the game, including while the computer is thinking.
  • The game still works in a private window (saving blocked).
  • Rotating a phone keeps the table sharp and the controls clear of the balls.
  • Switching views at any moment (even mid-shot) shows the same balls in the same places, and nothing resets; every game starts in Top view.
10Not in this version
  • Two people playing each other, on one device or online. This version is against the computer only.
  • The professional match limits on repeated hazards and cannons.
  • A free camera that can fly anywhere round the room: the two views (Top and Eye) cover what a player needs.
  • Jump shots, massé (big curling shots with a raised cue) and the "push stroke" rule.
  • Other cue games (snooker, pool). The table and physics would suit them later.
