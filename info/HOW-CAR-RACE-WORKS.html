What is this page and how does AI play a part?

This page explains how Game 1: Car Race works inside: how a flat canvas is made to look like a road racing towards you, how the car, the curves and the rivals behave, and what Claude Code (an AI coding agent) changed compared with the small open-source example it started from. Claude Code read that example, fixed its bugs and turned a driving demo into a full racing game, and a person tested every version on a computer and a phone. Players don't need any AI to play. The demos on this page use the same formulas and numbers as the game, so you can try them yourself.

1How a flat picture becomes a road (pseudo-3D)

There is no real 3D engine. The road is a long list of short, flat segments, each 200 units long, laid end to end. Every frame the game takes the next 300 segments in front of the car and works out where each one appears on the screen. Things far away are drawn smaller and closer to the horizon, things near are drawn bigger and lower down. Each segment becomes a trapezoid of grass, red-and-white rumble strip, grey road and white lane lines, and together they look like a road stretching away. This trick is called pseudo-3D, and classic arcade racing games used it long before computers could draw real 3D.

The projection: why far things look small

A line from the camera to a point on the road crosses the screen. The further away the point, the closer that crossing is to the horizon. Dividing by the distance z does exactly this, so a point twice as far away is drawn half as far below the horizon, and half as wide.

The same thing in code (from car-race.html) 
// how wide the view is: 100° field of view
cameraDepth = 1 / Math.tan(fieldOfView / 2);

// for each end of each road segment:
scale    = cameraDepth / z;      // far = small
screen.x = w/2 + scale * x * w/2;
screen.y = h/2 - scale * y * h/2;
screen.w = scale * roadWidth * w/2;

x, y and z are the point's position compared with the camera: sideways, up and down, and straight ahead. One division per point is all it takes.

CurvesThe road never really turns. Each segment has a curve number, and while drawing, every segment is shifted a little more sideways than the last, so the road appears to bend.
·
HillsEach segment has a height. Going up a hill, the far road rises on the screen; over a crest, the road behind it is hidden.
·
Hidden roadSegments are drawn near to far. Anything that would appear above the last thing drawn is behind a hill, so it is skipped.
·
Cars and treesDrawn far to near, after the road, so near things cover far things: the "painter's" order.

2Tracks are recipes

A track is built from a few helper functions that add stretches of road: addStraight, addCurve, addHill, addSCurves, addBumps and so on. Each stretch eases in to its bend or slope, holds it, then eases out again, so the road never jerks. These helpers come from the original example; the three tracks are new recipes built with them.

The scenery (trees, rocks, billboards, buildings) is placed by a random number generator with a fixed starting seed, so the same track always looks exactly the same. Coins, nitro cans and repair spanners sit in the centres of the 3 lanes and come back every lap.

3Driving: speed, steering and bends

The game moves forward in exact steps of 1/60 of a second (the same "fixed step" loop as the original). The car's position is how far along the road it is, plus how far left or right it is: playerX, where −1 and +1 are the edges of the road.

RuleValue
Top speed12,000 units a second: exactly one segment per step, shown as 240 km/h
Gas · brake · coasting+ top ÷ 5 · − top · − top ÷ 5, per second
⚡ NitroUp to 125% of top speed; a full bar lasts 2 seconds
On the grassSlows you to a quarter of top speed; trees and signs there cause a crash
DamageEach hard crash takes 7% off top speed (up to 3 times) until a 🔧 repair
🌧 RainSteering 12% weaker, bends push 30% harder, thicker fog
Steering and the push in a bend (from car-race.html) 
// you can only steer while moving
dx = dt * 2 * Math.min(1, speed / maxSpeed);
playerX += dx * steer;
// a bend pushes you outwards: more speed, more push
playerX -= dx * (speed / maxSpeed) * curve * 0.3;

That second line is the whole secret of cornering. Steering pulls the car one way, the bend pushes it the other, and the push grows with speed and with how sharp the bend is. Go into a hard bend too fast and steering can't win, so you slide onto the grass.

🧪 Try it: how fast can you take a bend?

The game's hired driver uses this rule: steering can pull back about 0.92 × grip, the bend pushes speed × curve × 0.3 (× 1.3 in the rain), so the top safe speed is 0.92 × grip ÷ (curve × push).

The bar is the safe speed out of 300 km/h (top speed with nitro).
4Rivals, positions and the hired driver

The 7 rivals each have their own top speed (spread evenly between two values set by Easy, Normal or Hard), a cornering skill, a launch and an aggression level. Every step each rival:

  • aims for its top speed, minus a slowdown in bends (good corner drivers lose less),
  • looks 10 segments ahead; if a slower car is in its lane, it moves to a free lane to overtake, or slows behind it,
  • sometimes moves across to block you when you close in from behind (the aggressive ones more often),
  • gets a gentle catch-up: a rival far ahead of you eases off a little and one far behind tries a little harder, so races stay close.

Positions come from one number per car: laps × track length + distance past the start line. Sorting the cars by it gives the live order (finished cars by their finishing time), which the HUD shows as "P3 / 8".

The hired driver (press H) drives your car and explains what it does in a coaching box, like a driving instructor:

  • it reads the next 60 segments, finds the bend that needs the lowest speed, and lifts off or brakes in time (it allows more speed for bends further away, because there is still room to slow down),
  • it changes lane early to pass slower cars, and detours for pickups when that lane is clear,
  • it steers a little into each bend to fight the push, and saves nitro for straights.

Races driven with its help are not added to the top 5. The same driver also races the self-playing demo on the portal (?demo) and the cool-down lap after the flag.

5Drawing and sound, all with code

Pictures. The original needed two picture files of cars and trees, and if one failed to load the game stayed on "Loading…" for ever. Now every car (seen from behind, in three steering poses), truck, palm tree, cactus, building, sign and coin is drawn with canvas shapes once, when the game starts, onto small hidden canvases. During the race they are just copied and scaled, which is fast.

Effects. The sky, hills and trees are wide strips that slide sideways in bends (parallax). There is screen shake on crashes, tyre smoke, grass dust, rain spray and streaks, speed lines with nitro, and at night every lamp and tail light is added with "lighter" blending so lights glow. Canvas pixels are capped at 1,600 across to keep big screens fast.

Sound. No sound files: everything is made with the browser's Web Audio. The engine is two oscillators through a filter, and its pitch climbs through 5 gears, dropping at each gear change. Tyre squeal, grass rumble and rain are looping filtered noise whose volume follows what the car is doing. Coins, nitro, crashes, laps and the countdown are short tones and noise bursts.

After the race the browser's built-in voice announces the result ("Game over. You won the race!"), then a little synthesised music loop plays. Nothing sounds until the first click or key press, and M mutes. You can also record a video of a race, with sound and the dials drawn in, and download it.

6What Claude Code changed compared with the original

The starting point was 234-car-race.html, the open-source "Javascript Racer v4" by Jake Gordon (MIT licence), from the flathtml 800 Examples collection. It had a beautiful road engine but was a driving demo, not a game: no rivals, no score, no end. Claude Code kept the engine (the projection, the road helpers, the fixed-step loop) and the credit, and changed the rest.
