What is this plan and how does AI play a part?

This page is the build plan for Game 4: Golf, the fourth game in the RAG Champ Games portal. Like the other games, it will be written by an AI coding agent (Claude Code) as one HTML file with plain JavaScript and the Canvas API. There are no frameworks, no build step and no server. Much of it reuses what was built and tested for Javelin: the flight physics, the sliders, the wind, the camera, the sound and the way the game is tested. PLEASE NOTE: players don't need any AI to play the game. The AI is used only to write the code.

⛳ Golf: Development Plan

Plan date: 22 Nov 2026 · Decisions agreed 22 Nov 2026 · Status: Phases 1–5 built 22 Nov 2026 (play Golf); Phase 6 done 23 Nov 2026 (How Golf works) · Javelin plan · How Javelin works

Goal: Build GAME-4-Golf/golf.html, an 18-hole golf game that can be played in a side view or a top-down view, switched with a button at any time. On each shot the player picks a club, turns to aim, sets the Angle and Power sliders and presses SWING. The ball flies along a real arc, bounces and rolls over the hills, and the aim is to sink it in the cup in as few strokes as possible. Every hole has a par (3, 4 or 5), and the round is scored against par like a real round of golf. To keep it unpredictable, every hole starts with a new random wind, and on a long hole it changes again every few minutes.

1How it plays
  1. Look at the hole. A short fly-over pans from the tee to the flag and shows the par, the length and the hazards. A strip map at the top always shows the whole hole and where the ball is.
  2. Choose a view with the ⇄ View button (or V), whenever you like. Top-down shows the hole from above, with its bends, trees and hazards; side shows the ground along your aim line, with its hills.
  3. Aim. Turn left or right (drag in top-down view, or the ←→ keys and turn buttons in either view). The game starts each shot aimed at the flag.
  4. Pick a club. Each club has its own loft (how high it sends the ball), top speed and how far the ball rolls after landing. The game suggests a club from the distance to the flag, but you can change it.
  5. Set Angle and Power and press SWING. The dotted aim guide shows the start of the flight in the current wind, like in Javelin, in both views.
  6. Watch the ball fly, bounce and roll, in whichever view you are using; you can switch during the flight too. The camera follows it and slows down when it nears the cup.
  7. Keep going from where it stopped until it drops in the cup. Then the scorecard shows the result: "Birdie! 3 on a par 4".

On the green the game switches to putting: you aim and set Power only, the ball rolls along the ground, and the slope of the green bends its speed and its line (top-down view shows the slope with arrows). A ball drops in only if it reaches the cup slowly enough; too fast and it skips over the hole.

3Wind that keeps you guessing
  • A new wind on every hole. At the start of each hole the wind gets a random speed (0 to 8 m/s) and a random compass direction, so it can be with you, against you, or across the hole.
  • Long holes change again. If a hole has been in play for 3 minutes, the wind changes once more, and again every 3 minutes after that.
  • Never mid-shot. If the time is up while the ball is moving, the change waits until it stops, so a shot is never spoiled by a change you couldn't see.
  • Warned and shown. A banner "The wind is changing!" and a whoosh, then the windsock, the flag on the green and the trees all swing to the new wind. In top-down view an arrow shows the full direction; in side view the pill shows the part with or against you, plus a small arrow for the crosswind.
  • Realistic effect. The same wind formula as Javelin, now in two directions: a headwind or tailwind changes the distance, and a crosswind curves the ball sideways, so you aim off to allow for it. High shots stay in the air longer and are pushed more.
  • Putts are not affected by wind, as in real golf.
  • Calm option: an "Easy" switch halves the wind; a "No wind" practice range lets players learn the clubs first.
How the wind is chosen (planned code) 
// at the start of every hole, and after 3 minutes on a hole:
function newWind() {
  wind.speed = round(random() * 8, 0.5);  // 0 to 8 m/s
  wind.dir   = random() * 360;            // any compass direction
  wind.next  = holeTime + 180;            // next change in 3 min
}
// each frame:
if (holeTime >= wind.next && ball.stopped) {
  newWind(); banner('The wind is changing!');
}

The hole clock counts only while the hole is being played (not on menus or when paused). A small clock next to the wind shows the time left on long holes.

★Meet Willy, the golfer

Willy is a cheerful young golfer, drawn in code from the owner's reference picture (the picture itself isn't used in the game). His look:

  • Cream golf cap with a slightly darker, curved brim
  • Brown hair poking out at the sides
  • Warm skin, rosy cheeks and a big, open smile
  • Red polo shirt with a collar; the game adds trousers, golf shoes and a club

In the game: a waggle while you aim, a full backswing and swing, a follow-through that holds the pose while the ball flies, a fist pump for a birdie, a jump for an eagle or hole in one, and a shrug and cap tip for a bogey.

5The course: 18 holes, par 72

Two nines with their own look. Each hole is a short recipe, like Javelin's levels: a centre line from the tee to the green (straight, or bending left or right as a dogleg), the width of the fairway along it, hills, and the hazards (bunkers, water, trees, out of bounds) placed by distance and side. The game builds the hole's map from the recipe, so holes are easy to read and tune. Lengths are in metres; par 3s are 120–180 m, par 4s 260–400 m and par 5s 420–520 m.

Front nine: Parkland. Green and friendly: gentle hills, a few trees and bunkers, one pond (hole 7, a par 3 over water), and two gentle doglegs (holes 5 and 8). Holes 1–3 are straight, easy openers so new players learn the clubs.

Back nine: Seaside cliffs. More wind feel, bigger hills, deep bunkers, a stream that crosses the fairway, and a cliff edge (out of bounds) on 15 and 18, and sharper doglegs (holes 11, 14 and 17) where a brave shot can cut the corner. Hole 18 is a long par 5 to finish, with the clubhouse behind the green.

  • Score is the total number of strokes, shown against par: 72 strokes is "E" (even), 75 is "+3", 70 is "−2".
  • Penalty strokes (water, out of bounds) count as strokes, as in real golf.
  • Saved in the browser: best 18-hole round, best front nine and back nine, best score on each hole, and the number of birdies and holes in one.
  • Round modes: Full 18, Front 9, Back 9, or any single hole for practice (single holes don't count for the best round).
  • Leave and come back: a round in progress is saved after every hole, so you can stop after hole 6 and carry on later.

8Deliverables and technical design
GAMES/

└── info/

  • Units: metres and seconds. The ball has 3 coordinates: along the hole, across it, and height. Each hole has a height map and a surface map (fairway, rough, sand…) on a 2 m grid, built from its recipe; heights in between are smoothed.
  • Two drawings of one world: the rules (sim) don't know about views. The side view draws the height map along the aim line (or the ball's path); the top-down view draws the surface map from above, shaded by height, with the ball raised by a shadow offset.
  • Ball in the air: Javelin's flight step in 3 directions, with 4 small steps per frame; the wind pushes along and across. The flight plays faster on screen (like Javelin's 2×) so long drives don't feel slow, without changing where the ball lands.
  • Landing: when the ball meets the ground, the speed is split into "along the slope" and "into the slope". The part into the slope bounces back, scaled by the surface's bounce; the part along it is slowed by its friction.
  • Rolling: rolling friction from the surface, plus gravity along the slope, so a ball rolls back down a steep bank. Below 0.05 m/s it stops.
  • The cup: 10.8 cm across. If the ball passes over it slower than about 1.5 m/s it drops in, faster and it skips over (or lips out).
  • Wind: chosen at the start of each hole and kept in the round's saved state with the hole clock, so leaving and coming back keeps the same wind.
  • No outside files, no network requests, works from file:///.

Build phases

Phase 1: One hole, the ball and the clubs

  • Canvas, fixed-step loop and the sim / game-ui split, copied from Javelin.
  • Height and surface maps, ball flight in 3 directions with Javelin's step, bounce, roll, slopes and the cup.
  • Both views and the ⇄ View button; aiming left and right; six clubs, Angle and Power sliders, SWING, the aim guide and the camera that follows the ball. The golfer is a simple stand-in figure until Phase 4.
  • Putting on the green. One test hole (with a dogleg) and a practice range with distance markers.
Done when: The ball always behaves the same way for the same settings with no wind, each club's carry matches the table within 10%, both views show the same shot, and a ball can be putted into the cup.

Phase 2: The course and scoring

  • All 18 holes as recipes, with rough, bunkers, water, out of bounds and trees.
  • Penalty strokes and drops, the 10-stroke pick-up, par, the scorecard and the round total.
  • Title screen, round modes (Full 18, Front 9, Back 9, single hole), saving a round in progress and best scores.
  • A Node solver plays every hole and reports the strokes it needs.
Done when: A full round can be played and scored correctly, and the solver can make par on every hole with no wind.

Phase 3: Wind on every hole

  • A random wind (speed and compass direction) at the start of every hole, and a change every 3 minutes on a long hole, never while the ball is moving.
  • Crosswind curving the ball; the windsock pill, the compass arrow in top-down view, the flag and trees bending, the "wind is changing" banner and sound.
  • Easy (half wind) and a no-wind practice range.
Done when: Every hole starts with a new wind, long holes change every 3 minutes, never mid-shot, and the solver can still make par or bogey on every hole in the strongest wind from every direction.

Phase 4: The golfer, the look and sound

  • Willy drawn in code from the owner's reference picture, from the side and from above, with a waggle, backswing, swing, follow-through and celebrations.
  • Parkland and Seaside scenery, the strip map, the hole fly-over, splashes and sand puffs, a ball trail.
  • Web Audio sounds and a calm music loop; Sound and Music switches.
Done when: Each nine has its own look, every event has a sound, and nothing sounds before the first tap.

Phase 5: Phones and polish

  • Phone layouts (upright and sideways), full screen, touch-friendly club buttons.
  • Slow motion as the ball nears the cup, "Birdie!" and "Hole in one!" moments.
  • Testing on a laptop and a phone, and fixes from that testing.
Done when: A full round plays smoothly on a phone, and no control covers the ball or the flag.

Phase 6: Portal and explainer

  • The "Game 4" card in GAMES/index.html becomes Golf, with Play, Plan and How it works buttons.
  • info/HOW-GOLF-WORKS.html: how a ball flies, bounces and rolls, with hands-on demos (pick a club and see the arc; curve a shot in a crosswind; putt on a slope), how one world is drawn in two views, how the wind changes, what was reused from Javelin, and a dated timeline.
  • Add the game to the table in CLAUDE.md.
Done when: The game opens from the portal, and the explainer links back to the game and the portal.
9Test checklist
  • Opens from file:/// in Chrome, Edge and Firefox with no console errors.
  • The same settings always give the same shot when the wind is the same.
  • The ball never falls through the ground, even on steep slopes at top speed.
  • Every hole can be finished at par by the solver with no wind, and at par or bogey in strong wind.
  • Strokes, penalties and the total against par add up correctly on the scorecard.
  • Every hole starts with a new wind; on a long hole it changes every 3 minutes, never while the ball is moving, and the clock stops when paused.
  • Switching views at any moment (even mid-flight) shows the same ball in the same place, and nothing resets.
  • Leaving mid-round and coming back keeps the hole, strokes and wind.
  • Switching tabs pauses the game; there are no stuck keys or sliders afterwards.
  • The game still works in a private window (saving blocked).
  • Rotating a phone keeps the picture sharp, and the controls never cover the ball.
10Decisions (agreed 22 Nov 2026)
  • Both views, switched at any time. A ⇄ View button (or V) flips between the side view and the top-down view whenever the player likes, before, during or after a shot. Because of the top-down view, the player can aim left and right, holes can bend, and the wind can blow across the hole.
  • A new golfer: Willy. Not Rocky. Willy is drawn in code from the owner's reference picture (cream cap, brown hair, big smile, red polo shirt); the picture itself is not used in the game. Phases 1–3 use a simple stand-in figure, and Willy is drawn in Phase 4.
  • A new wind on every hole. Random speed (0 to 8 m/s) and compass direction at the start of each hole, plus a change every 3 minutes on a long hole; never while the ball is moving.
  • Real-size distances. Holes of 120–520 m, with a strip map, so the numbers feel like real golf.
  • No tower physics. Golf needs no stacking or toppling, so Matter.js is not included; the ball and ground are written by hand.
  • Genre, not a copy. The game uses the familiar golf rules (18 holes, par, clubs, hazards), but has its own course, its own golfer (Willy), art and sounds. No course, characters, music or artwork from any existing golf game are used.
