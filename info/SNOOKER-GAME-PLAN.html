What is this plan and how does AI play a part?

This page is the build plan for Game 6: Snooker, the sixth game in the RAG Champ Games portal. Snooker is played on the same 12 ft × 6 ft table as Billiards, but with 22 balls: 15 reds, six colours and the white. Like the other games, it will be written by an AI coding agent (Claude Code) as one HTML file with plain JavaScript and the Canvas API. There is no build step and no server. It is built on Billiards and looks and plays like it: the same page, players, controls, Top and Eye views, ball physics, sound and way of testing. What is new is the snooker rules, the pack of reds, and a computer opponent that knows about breaks and safety play. PLEASE NOTE: players don't need any AI to play the game. The AI is used only to write the code (the computer opponent's shots are planned by ordinary code, not AI).

Goal: Build GAME-6-Snooker/snooker.html, a game of snooker seen from above on a full-size table with six pockets. Both players use the same white cue ball. You score by potting a red (1 point), then a colour (2 to 7 points), then a red again, and so on; when the reds are gone, the colours are potted in order from yellow to black. A run of pots is a break. Fouls give points to the other player. The player with more points when the last ball is potted wins the frame. You first choose your player from the same 10 cartoon portraits as Billiards, and play against the computer, Marker. There are two views, switched at any time: Top (the default) and the 3D Eye view from behind the cue ball.

The baulk line, the D and the spots are already in Billiards; snooker only adds the spots for the yellow, green and brown on the baulk line.

  • The pack: the reds are racked in rows of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 with the point red as close as possible to the pink without touching it. With 10 reds the rows are 1–4, with 6 reds 1–3.
  • Telling the balls apart on a phone: every ball has a thin dark rim, a shine and a soft shadow. The green is drawn darker and bluer than the cloth, and the brown clearly browner than a red. A "Numbers on balls" switch prints each ball's value on it (1 on reds), for small screens and for colour-blind players.
  • The ball on: the balls you may hit next glow softly, and the HUD says "On: a red" or "On: a colour" or "On: blue (5)".
  • Colours come back: while there are reds on the table, a potted colour is put back on its own spot. If its spot is covered, it goes on the highest-value free spot; if every spot is covered, as close as possible to its own spot, towards the top cushion.
  • A potted red stays down. So does a colour once all the reds are gone.
2Scoring and fouls
Order of play
  1. While reds are left: pot a red (1). Then you are on a colour of your choice; pot it for its value. Then a red again, and so on. Two or more reds potted in one shot all count.
  2. Then the colours in order: yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7. They stay down.
  3. The frame ends when the black goes in (or on a foul with only the black left). The higher score wins. If the scores are level, the black is put back on its spot and the player who wins a coin toss chooses who plays at it from the D; the first score or foul wins.
Choosing the colour (made simple)

In real snooker you name ("nominate") the colour you are going for when it isn't obvious. Here you don't have to: the first colour your white hits is the colour you chose. Pot that one and it counts; hit one and pot another and it is a foul.

Playing from hand from outside the D
  • How much: the value of the ball on, or of the highest ball involved in the foul (hit or potted), whichever is more, and never less than 4. So hitting the black first when on a red gives away 7.
  • After a foul, the other player chooses: play on from where the balls are, or make the fouler play again. A small box asks you; Marker chooses for himself.
  • After an in-off, the next player plays from the D.
  • Free ball: if a foul leaves you snookered (you can't hit both edges of any ball on in a straight line), the game says "Free ball". You may play any ball as if it were the ball on; it scores the same as the ball on.
  • Touching ball: if the white rests against a ball, the game says so, and you must play away from it. If that ball is on, it counts as already hit.
Help on screen
  • The HUD shows both scores, your break, the reds left and the points remaining. When a player is behind by more than is left, it shows "Snookers needed".
  • A Concede button in the menu gives the frame away. Marker concedes when he needs snookers and only the pink and black are left.

Left out: the "foul and a miss" rule (where a referee puts the balls back after a weak attempt to hit the ball on), the rule against snookering behind a free ball, the push shot and jump shot fouls, and the re-rack after a stalemate. They are hard to judge in a game against the computer and would hardly ever matter.

3How a turn plays
  1. The break-off: a coin toss decides who goes first. The first player places the white in the D and must hit a red. The usual shot is a thin hit on the end red of the back row, sending the white round the table and back into baulk.
  2. Place the ball if it is in hand: drag the white anywhere inside the D.
  3. Aim, read the guide, choose spin, set Power and SHOOT, exactly as in Billiards: drag on the table or use ←→ (Shift for fine aim), the fine-aim wheel, the dotted guide with the ghost ball and the hit ball's line, the big cue-ball picture for top, screw and side, the Power slider or pulling back the cue, and Space.
  4. The guide knows the ball on: it turns red with a cross when the first ball the white would hit is not on, so a foul never comes as a surprise.
  5. Watch the balls roll. Each pot pops up ("Red 1", "Black 7 · Break 24"), and a foul is announced ("Foul: hit the pink first, 6 to Marker").
Computer opponent: Marker
  • The same Marker as in Billiards (red curly hair, maroon waistcoat, yellow bow tie), now at the snooker table. He is the same on Easy, Medium and Hard; only his skill changes.
  • He tries many shots in the same sim as the real game: for each ball on and each pocket, a few paces and spins, plus a few thin and thick hits. Each try is scored by the points it makes and by where it leaves the white for the next ball (after a red: which colour is easiest, usually the black, pink or blue).
  • When there is no good pot he plays safe: a thin touch on a red that sends the white back to baulk, or a shot that leaves you snookered.
  • Easy tries few shots, has a big wobble in his aim and rarely plays safe; Medium a small wobble and some safety; Hard looks two shots ahead and plays good safety.
  • He "thinks" for a moment with the cue moving, so you can see what he is aiming at, and the search is spread over several frames so the screen never freezes.
Players
  • The first screen asks you to choose your player from the same 10 cartoon portraits as Billiards (Archie, Kumar, Lee, Jordan, Marcos, Linda, Maya, Zen, Rene, Maria). If you already chose one in Billiards, Snooker starts with them picked. "Change player" is on the title screen.
  • Your portrait and Marker's sit beside the table with the scores, as in Billiards. No players are drawn at the table (that was tried in Billiards and dropped as confusing seen from above).

The balls can't be twice real size this time. Billiards plays its 3 balls at twice real size (SIZE = 2) so they are easy to see on a phone. In snooker the pack has to fit between the pink and the black: from the pink to the back row and on to the black takes about 10.9 ball radii, and there are only 568 mm. That allows balls up to about 1.98 times real size, so at 2x the back row would overlap the black, and 22 big balls would also crowd the table.

Plan: balls at 1.6x real size (84 mm), which leaves a clear gap behind the pack and is still easy to see on a phone held sideways. The pockets are scaled the same way as the balls, so they have the same feel as a real snooker table. Both numbers are tried and tuned in Phase 1, as was done for Billiards (where careful pots were checked to go in about half the time).

Tuned in Phase 1 (24 Nov 2026): the same set of careful pots (random positions, a small wobble on the aim) went in 54% of the time with pockets at 1.6x, against 55% in Billiards; at 1.4x, 47%. So the pockets stay at 1.6x (POCKET = 1.6), and potting is about as hard as in Billiards.

6How the balls move (the physics)

The ball physics is copied from the sim script of Billiards, which is already tested: balls slide and then roll, top, screw and side, the 90° rule, cushions that side can change the angle off, pocket jaws, and exact hit times so nothing ever passes through anything. The cloth numbers start the same (sliding friction 0.2, rolling 0.01, ball-on-ball bounce 0.95, cushion bounce 0.75). What snooker adds:

  • Balls that touch. Billiards never has more than three balls, and they are hardly ever touching. A snooker pack is 15 balls packed together, so the break-off sends a hit through a chain of touching balls in one instant. The reds are racked with a tiny gap (about 0.1 mm) between them, so the sim handles the chain one exact hit at a time, in a fixed order, and the same break-off always spreads the pack the same way.
  • 22 balls instead of 3: 231 pairs of balls to check each small step instead of 3. Balls that are still are skipped, and the quick box test Billiards uses for cushions (skip what a ball cannot reach in this step) is used for pairs of balls too.
  • A white touching a ball is found and reported, so the touching-ball rule works.
  • Line of sight: a small helper checks whether the white can hit both edges of a ball in a straight line. It tells the referee when a player is snookered (for the free ball), lets the aim guide warn of a foul, and helps Marker choose safety shots.
  • Spots: the code that puts a colour back on its spot (or the next free one) also makes sure it never lands on top of another ball.
The shot record and the referee (planned code) 
// The sim records every touch in a shot:
{t:0.42, hit:'red7'}           // first ball hit
{t:0.90, cushion:'left'}
{t:1.31, pocket:'TR', ball:'red7'}
{t:2.05, pocket:'ML', ball:'white'}

// The referee reads only the record and the state:
referee(state, record) -> {
  points:  0,                  // what the striker scored
  foul:    {why:'in-off', value:4},
  next:    'cpu',              // whose turn
  on:      'red' | 'colour' | 'blue',
  respot:  ['pink'],           // colours to put back
  inHand:  true,               // white is played from the D
  freeBall:false, frameOver:false
}

As in Billiards, the referee has no drawing code, so every rule is tested in Node with set-up shots. The same shot with the same settings always gives exactly the same result, so Marker's planning matches what really happens.

Build phases

Phase 1: The table, the pack and the physics

  • snooker.html started from a copy of billiards.html: the page, the sim / eye3d / game-ui split, the loop, the controls and both views.
  • The 22 balls in their colours, the colour spots, racking 6, 10 or 15 reds; the ball size (1.6x) and pocket size tuned.
  • The physics changes: chains of touching balls, skipping still balls and far-apart pairs, the touching-ball check and the line-of-sight helper.
  • A Practice table: drag any ball anywhere and shoot, no rules, with a "Rack" button.
Done when: the same break-off always spreads the pack the same way; no ball ever overlaps another or passes through a cushion, even when a full-power shot hits the pack; a full-ball hit on one end of a straight line of touching reds sends only the far red away; and a frame's worth of shots runs smoothly on a phone.

Built 24 Nov 2026. The pockets stay at 1.6x (careful pots go in 54% of the time, as in Billiards). In Node, 10,000 random full-power shots into a full pack never left two balls overlapping or a ball off the table, and the same break-off always gave the same result. One change was needed: balls lying against each other, pushed together by their spin, made thousands of tiny hits under 1 mm/s (one frame worked out 1,157). Now two balls that meet that slowly part at 2 mm/s (V_SEP), and at most 60 hits are worked out in a 1/600 s step (MAX_HITS): a full-power shot into the pack makes about 50 hits, and a frame of play takes about 0.25 ms to work out on a laptop. Checked in Chrome at laptop and phone sizes; not yet tried on a real phone.

Phase 3: Snooker school and the Eye view

  • The Eye view with 22 balls: the ball colours and spots in 3D, the glow on the ball on, and the hand-drawn fallback updated the same way.
  • A "Numbers on balls" switch (both views).
  • A Snooker school of five set-up shots, each with a target circle for the white: (1) the long pot down the table, (2) red then stun for the black, (3) screw back for the pink, (4) the break-off back into baulk, (5) escape a snooker off one cushion. A sixth, timed challenge: clear the colours from yellow to black.
Done when: every Snooker school shot can be made, the aim guide in the Eye view lines up with the same ball as in Top view, and a whole frame can be played from the Eye view alone.

Built 24 Nov 2026. The drills were placed with a search in Node over aim, pace and spin, and 18 tests check them. Every drill can be made. The stun circle can't be reached with a middle-of-the-ball hit: over that distance a stun needs a touch of screw, so the hint says "just below the middle". The screw circle can only be reached with screw, and the long pot is mostly made with top. The clearance is judged by the frame's own referee (a miss or a foul ends it), with a clock and a saved best. The school uses the same guide and glow as a frame. The red ring warns before the white would hit the wrong ball, and only the white in hand can be moved, inside the D. "Numbers on balls" (N) works in the Top view and both Eye views. In the browser, drill 3 was made through the page and the game moved on to drill 4. The clearance scored the yellow and moved on to the green. With the Eye view only (the Top/Eye switch after ball in hand, the Power slider and SHOOT), four turns of a frame were played, Marker's included; a whole frame wasn't played through, because headless Chrome draws the Eye view at about 2 frames a second (Billiards is the same there). The guide in both views comes from the same firstContact, so it always picks the same ball.

Phase 4: Marker, the computer opponent

  • Shot search in the sim, built on Billiards' Marker: pots at each ball on into each pocket, a few paces and spins, scored by points and the leave for the next ball; choosing the best colour after a red.
  • Safety play and snookers; a proper break-off; choosing "play on" or "play again" after your foul; conceding.
  • Easy, Medium and Hard, with aim wobble and, on Hard, two-shot planning.
  • Fewer shots tried when there are many balls on, so the search stays fast with 15 reds.
Done when: in Node, over 30 six-red frames, Hard beats Easy in nearly all of them and Medium beats Easy most of the time; Hard makes breaks of 30 or more in full frames; and no shot takes more than 2 seconds to choose on a phone.

Built 24 Nov 2026. In Node, with Marker's planner playing both sides: over 30 six-red frames Hard beat Easy 30–0 (1,831 points to 288, 14 breaks of 30 or more, best 60), and Medium beat Easy 30–0 (1,409 to 516, best break 36). In five full 15-red frames Hard won all five, against Easy and Medium, with breaks of 30 or more (best 42). Run on its own on a laptop, a full search takes 0.4 s on average with 15 reds, and 1.5 s at most. In the game he thinks about 10 ms each frame and chooses when he has tried everything, or after 1.5 s of work or 2 s on the clock, whichever comes first, so on a phone his choice never takes more than 2 s; then the cue turns to his shot for about a second. On a slow device he tries fewer shots, in an order that still covers the break-offs, his likeliest pots and some safeties. Before this, the physics step was made 1.5 times faster without changing any result (290 shots compared ball by ball with the Phase 2 sim). In the browser, Hard Marker broke off and cleared up to win a six-red frame 64–6. Not yet tried on a real phone.

11Decisions (agreed 24 Nov 2026)

Agreed with the owner on 24 Nov 2026, one question at a time. The only change from the first draft is the default frame size (15 reds, not 6).

  • agreed Frame size: the full 15-red frame by default. 147 points on the table, about 25 minutes against the computer. Shorter frames of 10 reds (107) and 6 reds (75, about 10 minutes) are in the menu.
  • agreed Match length: one frame by default, with best of 3 and best of 5 in the menu.
  • agreed Ball size 1.6x real (not 2x as in Billiards, which does not fit the pack), with pockets scaled the same way; both tuned in Phase 1.
  • agreed Computer opponent: Marker again, the same character as in Billiards, at Easy, Medium or Hard. You choose your player from the same 10 portraits. No players at the table.
  • agreed Against the computer only. No two-player mode in this version.
  • agreed Rules made simple where it helps: the first colour hit is the chosen colour; free ball, touching ball, play on or play again and the respotted black are in; the "foul and a miss" rule and the others in section 10 are left out.
  • agreed Two views, as in Billiards: Top (the default) and the 3D Eye view with three.js (MIT), pasted into the file, with the hand-drawn fallback.
  • fixed Genre, not a copy. The game uses the standard rules of snooker, which belong to everyone, with its own table art, sounds and opponent. No graphics, names or music from any existing snooker game or TV coverage are used.
