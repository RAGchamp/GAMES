What is this plan and how does AI play a part?

This page is the build plan for Game 7: Play with a friend, the seventh entry in the RAG Champ Games portal. It is not a new sport. It takes the two cue games already built, Billiards and Snooker, and makes versions of them where you invite a friend. Each of you plays on your own computer or phone, wherever you are, and your friend takes the place of Marker, the computer opponent. Like the other games, it is written by an AI coding agent (Claude Code) in plain JavaScript. What is new is a small amount of code that passes each shot between the two players through an online "room". PLEASE NOTE: players don't need any AI to play the game. The AI is used only to write the code.

Goal: Build GAME-7-With-friend/, holding a Billiards and a Snooker page that two people play against each other over the internet. One player creates a game and gets a short room code and link, which they send to a friend (WhatsApp, text or email). The friend opens the link, signs in with Google, chooses their player from the same 10 portraits, and the match starts. Each player sees the table on their own screen and picks their own view (Top or Eye). When it is your friend's turn you watch them aim and shoot, the way you watch Marker now. The rules, scoring, fouls and frames are exactly the ones in the existing games. The original Billiards and Snooker stay as they are, against Marker.

1How the two computers talk

A web page can't reach another person's computer by itself, so both pages connect to a shared room held by an online service. Each player writes their moves into the room and reads the other player's moves from it. Nothing else is needed: the games stay plain web pages, and no server of our own has to be kept running.

Why so little is sent: both games are turn-based and the physics is already kept apart from the drawing code (the sim script). simulate(state, 'white', angle, power, maxT, spin) plays a whole shot from a handful of numbers. So a shot is sent as about five numbers, and the other screen plays it out with the same code. About a dozen small messages go each way per shot. That is tiny, and it works on a weak phone signal.

2How a turn plays
  1. Striker lines up. Their aim, power and spin are sent about 10 times a second (only when they change), so the other screen shows the cue moving, as it does for Marker now.
  2. Striker with ball in hand drags the white. Its place is sent the same way.
  3. Striker presses SHOOT. The shot is sent: where the white is, angle, power, spin and a move number.
  4. Striker's screen plays the shot and the referee scores it (applyShot, unchanged). The result is sent: every ball's final place and the whole match state (scores, break, ball on, free ball, whose turn, frames).
  5. Watcher plays the same shot from the shot numbers, so they see the balls move. When the balls stop, any ball that is off slides to the place the result says (in about a quarter of a second), and the striker's score is taken as final.
  6. Whoever's turn it is now goes on from step 1. After a foul, the player who didn't foul gets the Play on / Play again choice, and their answer is sent as a move.
Why the striker's screen has the final say: different browsers (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, phone or laptop) can work out sin and cos very slightly differently. In a pack of 15 reds a difference of a billionth of a millimetre can grow into a different result. So the watcher's playback is for show, and the striker's result is what counts. Usually the two agree to the millimetre anyway; when they don't, the watcher's balls glide the last bit instead of jumping.

Chance decided by one side: the toss for who breaks, the respotted-black toss (applyShot uses rnd) and Snooker's colour respotting all happen on the striker's screen (the creator's for the opening toss) and arrive with the result, so both screens always agree.

3Choosing the online service
OptionHow it worksGoodNot so good
A. Google Firebase
chosen		Both pages read and write the room in Firebase's Realtime Database. It tells each page at once when something changes.Reliable on any network; free plan is far more than two friends need (100 people connected at once, 10 GB a month); offers sign-in (Google or "guest"); shows when someone has left.The owner creates a free Firebase project (about 15 minutes, guided). The pages load Google's Firebase script from the web. The game must be opened from a web address, not a file on disk.
B. Direct link
(WebRTC, using PeerJS)		The two browsers connect straight to each other after a short introduction through a free "broker".No account to create; nothing stored anywhere.The free broker is best-effort; some home, school and office networks block direct links (a relay server would cost money); no sign-in; harder to rejoin after a page reload.
C. Our own small server
(Node + WebSockets)		A tiny relay hosted on Render, Fly.io or Cloudflare.Full control; the server could even check every shot.Something to deploy, watch and possibly pay for; most work.

The room code is written so the service can be swapped: all talk with the service goes through one small file (online.js, section 7) with five functions: create room, join room, send move, on move and on friend left/back. Moving from A to B or C later changes only that file.

4The screens

On the friend's turn: "Maya is lining up…". A small connection light by each name (green: here, amber: slow, grey: left). Three quick reactions to tap (👏 Good shot, 😮 Unlucky, 😄 Ha!), shown as a short pop-up on the other screen.

Moves are numbered and only ever added, never changed, so a page that reconnects reads the moves it missed and catches up. Each page checks that a move is the one it expects (the right number, from the player whose turn it is) and ignores anything else.

Deleting old rooms: Firebase's Realtime Database never deletes anything by itself, and a scheduled clean-up job needs the paid plan. So each time a room is created, the page also deletes any rooms more than 24 hours old. The rules allow a delete only for rooms that old. This keeps everything on the free plan.

6What is reused, and what is new
  • reused The whole sim script of each game: table, balls, physics, the referee (referee, applyShot), frames, free ball, respotted black, concede. Unchanged.
  • reused The eye3d Eye view, the Top view, the controls, sounds, pop-ups, phone layouts and the 10 portraits.
  • changed The players are still 'you' and 'cpu' inside the code, so the sim stays the same. On both screens 'you' is the creator and 'cpu' is the friend, so the two match states are always the same and can be compared directly. Each page knows which one it is (me), and the game-ui asks "is m.turn me?" instead of "is it 'you'?". Names, portraits and messages ("You need snookers", "Maya needs snookers") are shown from the viewer's side. In Billiards this also keeps the balls right: the creator plays the white, the friend the yellow, on both screens. Every "Marker" on screen becomes the friend's name.
  • changed The spot where Marker takes his turn (game.phase = 'cpu' in game-ui) becomes 'friend': wait for the friend's moves and play them out. Marker's own shot search (cpuPlanner) isn't called.
  • changed Marker's choices (play on or play again, conceding) come from the friend instead.
  • new online.js, the room screens, the connection lights, reconnecting, quick reactions and rematch.
  • Saving: the original games save under billiards.save.v1 and snooker.save.v1. The online pages read the chosen portrait from there (so you don't choose it again) but keep their own save (friend.save.v1: your portrait, the last room, the results against each friend), so an online match never overwrites a game against Marker. Results are kept per friend's sign-in ID (shown with the portrait they last used), so two different friends who both play as "Maya" stay separate.

  • Copies, not changes: the two game pages start as copies of billiards.html and snooker.html, so the games against Marker can't be broken by this work. The only change to the originals is a small Play a friend link on their title screens.
  • Kept in step by a build script: F-PAD/build/make-friend.js copies the sim and eye3d scripts (and the three.js line) from the original games into the friend pages, leaving their own game-ui alone. After a physics or rule fix in an original game, running the script brings it into the friend copy too. CLAUDE.md will say so.
  • One shared file for the room code, online.js, used by both pages, so it is written and fixed once. (The games are otherwise one file each; this is the one exception.)
  • Firebase project in the owner's own Google account, on the free plan. Its public web settings (meant to be visible in a web page) sit at the top of online.js, in the public GitHub repo.
  • Safety: strict database rules allow only signed-in players to make a room, only the two players in a room to write to it, only adding moves (never changing old ones), small moves, and deleting a room only once it is 24 hours old. On top of that, Firebase App Check lets only pages served from the game's GitHub Pages address use the database. Testing on localhost uses an App Check debug token.
  • Sign-in: Google sign-in (Firebase Authentication). Each player's hidden sign-in ID is what the room rules check, so signing in on another device with the same account takes back your seat. Google is used only to sign in: the other player sees just your chosen portrait and its name, and your Google name, email and photo are never written to the room.
  • Hosted on GitHub Pages from the RAGchamp/GAMES repo. Firebase only works from an https:// page (or http://localhost for testing), not from a file opened on disk, so both players open the GitHub Pages address. That address is also added to Firebase's allowed sign-in domains and to App Check.
  • Test hooks as in the other games: ?room=CODE (join), ?debug (readout and window.__friend), and ?fake, which swaps online.js for a stand-in that passes moves between two tabs on the same computer (no internet), so most of the work can be tested without Firebase.

Done when: two browser windows, signed in with two Google accounts, join the same room by code; a move sent by one arrives at the other in well under a second, in order; closing one window shows "left" on the other after the grace time, and reopening it catches up on any moves it missed; a third account can't join a full room; a page not on the GitHub Pages address (or localhost with the debug token) is refused by App Check; creating a room deletes rooms more than 24 hours old.

Done when: the friend's cue moves smoothly (no jumps) on a slow connection; locking a phone for a few seconds doesn't show "lost the connection"; turning off a phone's Wi-Fi mid-shot and back on returns it to the right table with the right scores; reloading either page mid-game carries on the game; signing in on a second device with the same account takes back the seat.

8Test checklist
  • Create, share and join on a laptop and on a phone; a wrong or old code gives a clear message.
  • Both screens agree on every ball, score, break and frame after every shot, in both games (scripted 200-shot tests and played frames).
  • Each player can switch between Top and Eye view without affecting the other.
  • Moves can't be made out of turn: pressing SHOOT on the friend's turn does nothing; a move with the wrong number is ignored.
  • Reloading a page, closing the lid of a laptop, or losing the phone signal and coming back all return to the same table.
  • Rooms more than 24 hours old are deleted when a new room is created; a third person can't join or write to a room; a page not served from GitHub Pages is refused by App Check.
  • No Google name, email or photo is written to the room or shown to the other player.
  • Two players with the same portrait can be told apart; results are kept per friend.
  • Running make-friend.js after a change to an original game's sim brings the change into the friend copy, and the scripted tests still pass.
  • The original Billiards and Snooker are unchanged apart from the Play a friend link, and still play against Marker.
  • Firebase use stays well inside the free plan (checked in its dashboard after the Phase 5 test).
9Not in this version
  • Playing strangers, finding an opponent, rankings or leaderboards.
  • More than two players, or people watching a match.
  • Typed chat, voice or video (the three quick reactions only).
  • A shot clock (you can take as long as you like).
  • A paid Firebase plan or scheduled server jobs.
  • Checking shots for cheating: the striker's screen is trusted, which is fine between friends.
  • Marker in the online pages, or two people taking turns on one device.
  • Online versions of the other games (Car Race, Chintu, Javelin, Golf).
